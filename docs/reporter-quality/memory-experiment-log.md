# Reporter memory experiment log

This is a public-safe summary. Private articles, participant identifiers, source
snapshots, traces, accounting records and artifact locations are intentionally
kept outside the repository. Results below are individual stochastic samples,
not estimates of general model superiority.

## Baseline and acquisition prompt

The original full-season baseline missed an available historical acquisition in
the final article. A strengthened acquisition cue did not recover it and stopped
at the application's serialized-input byte guard before submission. This was
not evidence that the provider's context-token limit had been reached.

## Flash transport and headroom

An original-prompt Flash comparison at an 8,192-token output cap stopped at the
per-response output limit. A separately labeled 32,768-token follow-up completed.
Reasoning shares the completion allowance. Preserve the failed equal-limit
sample; the follow-up is not an equal-output-limit model comparison.

The subsequent unchanged Flash season completed all 17 requests from empty
memory. It recovered historical acquisition information, including via storyline
recall followed by source verification. That does not prove direct retrieval of
the original event card. Repeated source transactions acquired duplicate event
identities, and some storylines replaced an answered question with a new one.

## Question identity treatment

One closeout paragraph asks the reporter to preserve each arc's unresolved
question, resolve it when answered, and give a genuinely separate durable question
its own identity. A matched empty-memory season completed all 17 articles. It
showed a clear season-long question carried to a source-supported resolution and
fewer clear question replacements in a manual audit. These observations are
promising but mixed: case selection differed, compound acquisition questions
remained difficult to assess, and active memory increased recall pressure.
Infrastructure failures and bounded checkpoint recoveries were preserved.

An initial audit overstated one allegedly unsupported closure. Corrected source
arithmetic supported the narrow scoring-floor interpretation; the broader
acquisition and future-return question remained scope-sensitive. This case must
remain ambiguous rather than count as an unequivocal treatment failure or win.
No duration quota or universal closure criterion is justified by these samples.

## Partial-question follow-up: not promoted

A three-run gate compared control and candidate at the same prior checkpoint and
checked a later legitimate closure. The fresh control already left the disputed
question active. The candidate also left it active but introduced an additional
criterion not established in its prior question. The legitimate closure passed,
but the gate did not establish the intended improvement. No full-season follow-up
was launched, and this rejected instruction is not included in the candidate.

The instruction became visible only during closeout after article submission.
Article and research differences in those runs therefore cannot be attributed to
it. A separate observation about weekly standings decisions remains a shelved
hypothesis until the configured source rule is verified.

identities in both Flash seasons. A separate candidate addresses that write
boundary. Its deterministic replay result does not establish better article
quality or storyline lifecycle. Keep that change separate from this prompt.
